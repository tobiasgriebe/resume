## Role Summary

Director-level role setting technical direction for high-performance, latency-sensitive backend systems underpinning Deutsche Bank's electronic FX/Fixed Income trading platform. Leads a globally distributed engineering team delivering front-to-back trading solutions, owns architecture strategy and governance, mentors senior engineers, and drives AI-assisted engineering adoption. No salary stated.

---

## Overall Fit: LOW-MEDIUM (~35%)

The leadership shape of the role — Director owning technical vision, leading a distributed org, mentoring, hiring, architecture governance — matches your track record closely. But the posting is not a pure people-leadership Director role; it wants a technical player-coach with current, expert-level hands-on Core Java skills in a low-latency trading context. Your Java/Kotlin experience is real but dates from before your move into full-time engineering leadership (~2016 onward), and you have no electronic trading / market-latency domain background. This is a harder gap to bridge in a cover letter than a pure domain gap, because it's a stated "must-have" technical bar, not a nice-to-have.

This describes a technical architect / staff-engineer-turned-Director who still codes at expert level in a performance-critical domain.

- This is a harder gap than the banking-regulatory gap seen in the DKB assessment: it's a stated hard skill requirement, not a contextual/domain one, and it's difficult to credibly narrate around without recent hands-on evidence.

Unlike the DKB banking-regulatory gap (bridgeable via GoBD/compliance-adjacent framing), electronic trading has its own technical vocabulary (market venues, order routing, latency budgets) that isn't approximated by other fintech experience.

## Key Risks

1. **Hands-on Core Java bar is explicit and repeated** — this reads as a genuine screening criterion, not boilerplate; a technical interview would likely probe multithreading/performance topics you haven't worked hands-on in ~10 years.
2. **No electronic trading domain experience** — market microstructure, latency-sensitive architecture, and trading-specific regulatory context (MiFID II, market abuse controls) are unfamiliar.
3. **Player-coach framing** — the role wants someone who still codes at expert level while leading, a hybrid you've moved away from since 2016.
4. **DB Director banding** — verify actual comp before investing time; "Director" in an investment bank is not equivalent to "Director" title inflation seen in tech scale-ups.

## Recommendation

**Weak candidate on the stated technical bar — pass unless you're prepared to reposition as hands-on again.** The leadership dimensions are a strong match, but the posting's core technical requirement (expert hands-on Core Java in a low-latency trading context) is a genuine gap, not a narrative problem to smooth over. Applying risks an early technical screen rejection rather than a leadership-fit rejection, which is a worse outcome for calibrating DB as a target. If you have more recent hands-on Java/performance engineering experience not reflected in the current CV, that would change this assessment materially — otherwise, better DB entry points would be a role titled around engineering leadership/delivery rather than one this explicit about hands-on systems expertise.
